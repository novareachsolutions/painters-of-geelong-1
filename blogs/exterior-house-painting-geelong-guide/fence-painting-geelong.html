Fence Painting in Geelong: Timber & Colorbond, Paint vs Stain

August 3, 2026 Painters of Geelong 7 min read

Paint or stain for timber, and a completely different rulebook for Colorbond steel — what each surface actually needs, and what it costs to get right.

Quick answer: Timber fences can be painted or stained depending on the look you want; Colorbond steel needs a specific metal primer and should never be heavily sanded. Geelong fence painting runs $18–$30/m for timber and $40–$50/m for Colorbond. Full cost guide →

Timber Fences: Paint or Stain?

These solve different problems, and it's the first question our exterior fence painting services talk through with homeowners. Paint forms an opaque film that fully hides the timber's grain and any past repairs or colour variation — it gives you the widest colour choice and the most "finished" look, but shows scratches and wear more visibly over time and eventually needs stripping back for a repaint. Stain soaks into the timber rather than sitting on top, so the natural grain still shows through — lower maintenance long-term (no peeling film to fail), but a narrower, more natural colour range.

For a Geelong paling or picket fence in reasonable condition, either works well, and colour selection often ends up being the deciding factor rather than performance. For an older fence with mismatched or repaired sections, paint is usually the better call since it evens everything out and gives more consistent weather protection across patched areas; stain will show every patch and repair.

Preparing a Timber Fence for Paint

  • Wash down to remove dirt, moss, and grime — a pressure washer on a low setting works well for palings.
  • Scrape and sand back any loose or flaking old paint to a sound edge.
  • Replace or repair any rotten palings or posts before painting — paint won't stop rot that's already active.
  • Spot-prime bare or repaired timber before applying two topcoats of an exterior acrylic paint.

Painting a Colorbond Steel Fence

Colorbond is a different job entirely — it's a factory pre-painted steel product with a baked-on finish, so the process and the products are specific to metal, not timber. It's a common ask for outdoor painting services Geelong properties get quoted alongside a full exterior repaint, and one exterior painters Geelong crews handle very differently to a timber fence.

  1. Clean thoroughly. Remove dirt, cobwebs, and the chalky residue that forms as the factory finish weathers — this "chalking" is usually the visual sign a Colorbond fence is due for a repaint.
  2. Light scuff only — never heavy sand. A fine abrasive pad is enough to help the primer key on. Aggressive sanding can damage the anti-corrosive zinc layer beneath the factory coating.
  3. Use a metal-specific primer. An etch primer or a Colorbond-compatible self-priming paint is essential — standard exterior wall paint won't adhere properly to the smooth, non-porous factory finish.
  4. Apply two coats of a 100% acrylic exterior coating formulated for metal. This flexes with the steel as it heats and cools, avoiding the cracking that a rigid, cheaper paint develops over time.
Coastal note: Weather-resistant exterior paint Torquay jobs on Colorbond fences chalk and fade fastest, since they cop salt spray directly off the surf coast. Beach house painters Ocean Grove homeowners call on see a little more shelter from the same salt exposure, but it still shortens the repaint cycle compared to inland suburbs. New estates tend to have Colorbond fencing installed fresh, so a light clean is often all that's needed for the first several years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to get a fence painted?

Timber fence painting in Geelong runs roughly $18–$30 per metre, while Colorbond steel fence painting costs more at $40–$50 per metre due to the specialist metal primer required. A standard 20-metre boundary fence typically costs $360–$600 for timber or $800–$1,000 for Colorbond.

Is it worth painting the fence?

Yes, if the fence is structurally sound but faded or worn — a repaint is far cheaper than replacement and can add years of life. It's not worth it if posts or palings are rotten, warped, or falling apart; painting won't fix structural failure.

How much does it cost to paint a Colorbond fence?

Professional Colorbond fence painting in Geelong typically costs $40–$50 per linear metre, reflecting the specialist metal primer and acrylic topcoat needed. A standard 20-metre boundary fence generally falls between $800 and $1,000.

Can you just paint over old fence paint?

Only if the existing paint is sound and well-adhered. Loose, flaking, or chalky old paint needs to be scraped and sanded back first, or the new coat will simply fail along with the layer beneath it. A quick adhesion test — pressing tape onto the surface and pulling it off — shows whether the old paint is holding.

How often should a timber fence be painted?

Most timber fences need repainting every 4–7 years, noticeably more often than a house exterior, since fences sit closer to the ground, get less protective eaves coverage, and take more direct weather exposure on both sides. Fences backing onto full afternoon sun or a sprinkler line often sit at the shorter end of that range.
