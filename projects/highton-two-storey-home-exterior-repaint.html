Residential Exterior Painting · Highton

Exterior Painting for a Two-Storey Highton Home

This residential exterior painting project involved repainting a large two-storey home in Highton, covering its rendered and weatherboard-style surfaces, windows, shutters, gutters and downpipes.

The Challenge

The Challenge of Painting a Large Two-Storey Home

This Highton property is a genuinely large two-storey home, with a curved gable roofline, a mix of rendered and weatherboard-style surfaces, multiple windows and shutters running across both levels, and a balcony structure extending off the rear. None of that would be unusual on its own, but this home also sits directly beside a swimming pool and a paved outdoor entertaining area.

That changes the brief. A wall you only glance at from the street is one thing — a wall you're looking at every time you're in the pool or sitting out the back is another. Every surface transition, every window edge, every run of gutter down two storeys was going to be seen up close, repeatedly, not just from a passing car. It's the kind of property that comes up often for exterior painters in Highton, where larger homes with pools and outdoor entertaining areas are a common feature.

Scope of Work

Exterior Painting Scope

  • Upper exterior walls finished in a light, neutral tone
  • Lower exterior wall finished in a contrasting dark charcoal tone
  • Gutters and downpipes finished to complement the exterior
  • Windows, shutters and the balcony structure detailed as part of the finish

Our Approach

Treating a Big Home Like Several Small Ones

A property this size doesn't get finished in one pass. It gets broken into sections, and each one gets the same level of care as the last, which is really what separates a considered exterior house painting service from a job that just moves fast.

01

Walk the full property

With a home this size, understanding every wall, level and surface type before starting is what keeps the finished result consistent from one end to the other.

02

Plan the tonal split

Deciding exactly where the light upper tone meets the dark lower band matters — that line runs the length of the house, so it needs to sit level and clean throughout.

03

Work across every surface type

The rendered sections and the weatherboard-style cladding both needed to read as part of the same finish, not two different jobs stitched together.

04

Detail the openings and fixtures

Windows, shutters, gutters and downpipes running down two storeys all needed clean, consistent lines around them.

05

Finish along the poolside wall

The wall running beside the pool and paved entertaining area was given the same level of attention as the rest of the home, since it's the section seen most often.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Why use two different tones on a home's exterior, light upper and dark lower?

Splitting a facade into a lighter upper section and a darker lower band is a common way to add visual definition to a tall or multi-level home, helping break up a large expanse of wall into more clearly defined sections.

What makes exterior painting more complex on a multi-level home?

A multi-level home typically has more wall height, more window and shutter openings, and often a mix of surface types such as render and weatherboard-style cladding, all of which need to be planned and finished consistently across a larger, more varied facade.

Does a pool or outdoor entertaining area affect how an exterior repaint is approached?

A pool or entertaining area sitting directly against the house means that exterior wall is seen up close and often, so a neat, consistent finish along that section matters just as much as the rest of the property.

How do painters keep clean lines between rendered and weatherboard-style surfaces?

Where different surface types meet, clean preparation and careful paint application help create a sharp transition, so the change from one surface to another reads as intentional rather than uneven.

Why does colour placement matter around windows, gutters and downpipes?

Windows, gutters and downpipes are some of the most visible detail points on a facade, so keeping the paint line clean around them has a noticeable effect on how tidy the whole exterior appears.

The Result

One Home, Read as a Single Considered Whole

The finished exterior gives this Highton property a genuinely cohesive appearance, despite its size and the range of surfaces involved. The light upper facade and the dark lower band aren't just a colour choice — they give the eye a clear way to read the building, breaking a tall, substantial home into proportions that feel more approachable rather than one flat, overwhelming wall of colour.

Along the poolside wall in particular, the finish reads as neat and even up close, not just from across the yard — which matters for a section of the house that gets used and looked at daily. It's the kind of result residential exterior painters aim for on a property where the outdoor entertaining area is as much a part of the home as any room inside it.
