Exterior House Painting in Torquay: White & Charcoal Character Home

A weatherboard bungalow with a feature gable dormer, finished in a crisp white body with contrasting charcoal window frames and trims — a classic coastal palette that lets the home's character shine.

Category
Exterior Painting
Location
Torquay, VIC

The Challenge

A weatherboard character home, ready for a fresh finish

This weatherboard home in Torquay shows off a classic Californian bungalow silhouette — a steep gable roof, a feature dormer window upstairs, and a welcoming front porch. Homes of this age and style typically call for careful attention to timber cladding and trims to keep their character sharp, and this one has come up with a fresh, coordinated exterior colour scheme.

The home features a corrugated metal roof in a dark grey tone, a red brick chimney left in its natural, unpainted finish, and a matching brick base to the front porch and steps. A white porch post frames the entry, and the upstairs gable dormer window is picked out in the same charcoal used on the ground-floor window frames, tying the two levels together visually.

The front garden includes an established mixed garden bed with bird of paradise plants, low shrubs along the fence line, and a well-kept lawn — all contributing to a tidy, well-presented street frontage that complements the fresh paintwork.

The Scope

What the finished exterior includes

  • Weatherboard wall cladding finished in a crisp white
  • Window frames and sills, including the upstairs dormer window, painted in a contrasting dark charcoal
  • Front porch post finished in matching white
  • Front security door finished in a dark tone consistent with the window frames
Note: the brick chimney and porch base were left in their natural, unpainted finish — a deliberate choice that lets the exposed brickwork contrast with the painted weatherboard body.

Our Approach

What a finish like this typically involves

Weatherboard homes in coastal areas like Torquay need careful preparation to hold a finish this clean. Here's the general process behind a result like this one.

01

Wash down the cladding

Weatherboards are washed to clear away salt, dust and grime built up from the coastal air, giving the surface a clean base to work from.

02

Scrape and sand back

Any flaking, peeling or uneven paint is scraped and sanded back to a sound surface, so the new coat has something solid to grip onto.

03

Repair and fill

Cracks, gaps and any minor timber damage are filled and smoothed, keeping trims like the window frames and porch post crisp and even.

04

Prime bare timber

Exposed or repaired timber is primed before topcoats go on, helping the paint adhere properly and last against coastal weather.

05

Apply the colour scheme

The weatherboard body is coated in the chosen white, with window frames, sills and trims picked out separately in a contrasting charcoal.

06

Cut in the detail

Edges around windows, the porch post and the front door are cut in cleanly, giving the finished home the crisp, defined lines seen here.

Common Questions

Questions about weatherboard exterior repaints

Why paint window frames and trims a different colour to the walls?

A contrasting trim colour, like the charcoal used here against white weatherboard, defines the home's architectural features — window frames, the gable dormer and the porch — giving the exterior a sharper, more considered look than a single flat colour.

Should a brick chimney be painted to match the rest of the exterior?

Not necessarily. Many homeowners choose to leave a brick chimney or porch base unpainted, letting the natural brick texture and tone contrast with the painted weatherboard body, as seen on this home.

How often does weatherboard cladding need repainting in coastal areas?

Coastal exposure to salt air and UV tends to wear paint faster than inland areas, so weatherboard homes in places like Torquay generally benefit from repainting somewhat more often than the same home would need further from the coast.

Can a white and charcoal colour scheme suit a character weatherboard home?

Yes — a crisp white body with dark charcoal trims is a popular pairing for Californian bungalow and other character-style weatherboard homes, offering a clean, contemporary contrast while still suiting the home's original architectural style.

What's involved in preparing weatherboard cladding before painting?

Preparation typically includes washing down the cladding, scraping and sanding back any flaking paint, filling cracks or minor damage, and priming bare or repaired timber before topcoats are applied.

The Result

A tidy, well-presented street frontage

The crisp white weatherboard body reads as bright and well-maintained, while the charcoal window frames and trims add a contemporary edge without competing with the home's original character. Left untouched, the exposed brick chimney and porch base provide a warm, textural contrast to the painted surfaces.
