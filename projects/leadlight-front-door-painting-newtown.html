Door & Trim Painting for a Federation-Era Home in Newtown

A Federation-era leadlight front door and matching sidelight in Newtown, carefully repainted in charcoal and white without a mark on the original glass or brass hardware.

Category
Door & Trim Painting
Property
Federation-Era Character Home
Location
Newtown, VIC

The Challenge

Protecting Original Leadlight Glass During Painting

This isn't just any front door. The upper panel holds an original leadlight glass feature — a floral Art Nouveau design in leaded glass that's almost certainly as old as the house itself. Beside it sits a matching sidelight with a textured glass panel, and the door itself carries ornate brass hardware that looks original too.

None of that could be risked. A front door repaint on a home like this isn't really about the timber — it's about painting confidently right up to the edge of glass and brass without ever touching either. Get too close and you risk paint on the leadlight; stay too far back and the finished line looks careless.

What Was Painted

What We Painted

  • Front door repainted in a deep charcoal tone
  • Door panels and mouldings finished in crisp white
  • Sidelight frame and lower panel painted to match the door
  • Original leadlight glass and brass hardware left untouched

Our Approach

Careful Preparation and Detail Painting

A front door is a small job by area, but not by attention required. This one called for the same care a proper door and trim painting service should bring to any job, just concentrated onto a single doorway rather than a whole house of skirting and architraves.

01

Assess the Door and Sidelight

The timber, glass and hardware were assessed together, since each needed a different approach before painting began.

02

Protect the Leadlight and Hardware

The leadlight glass panel, the sidelight's textured glass, and the brass handle and knob were carefully masked off before any paint went near them.

03

Prepare the Timber

The door and sidelight panelling were prepared to give the new coats a clean, even surface to bond to.

04

Apply the Charcoal and White Finish

The main door was painted in charcoal, with the raised panels and mouldings picked out separately in white for contrast.

05

Finish the Sidelight to Match

The sidelight frame and lower panel were finished in the same palette, so the whole entry reads as one considered feature.

06

Reveal the Glass and Hardware

Masking was removed to reveal the original leadlight and brass fittings exactly as they were, now framed by a completely fresh finish.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a front door be repainted without damaging its original leadlight glass?

Yes. Original leadlight or stained glass panels can be masked off before painting, so the timber frame around them gets a fresh finish while the glass and leadwork stay completely untouched.

Should brass door furniture be removed before painting?

Removing or carefully masking handles, knobs and locks before painting protects the original hardware and makes it much easier to achieve a clean, sharp line around each fitting.

What colour works well on a Federation-era front door?

A deep, saturated colour such as charcoal is a popular choice for Federation and Edwardian-era doors, since it provides a strong contrast against white trims and lets original features like leadlight glass and brass hardware stand out.

Does a sidelight window need to be painted to match the front door?

Matching the sidelight's painted panel to the front door helps the whole entry read as one considered feature, rather than the door and sidelight looking like two separate, unrelated elements.

Is it worth repainting just a front door rather than the whole exterior?

Yes, particularly on a character home where the door is already a standout feature. A well-executed door repaint can noticeably lift a home's street presence even when the rest of the exterior isn't due for attention yet.

The Result

The Glass Gets to Do the Talking Again

With the repaint finished, the leadlight glass is the first thing you notice again — not the tired paintwork that used to sit around it. Charcoal against crisp white gives the entry a confident, contemporary edge, and the deep tone makes the coloured glass and brass hardware read even more clearly against it than they did before.

It's the kind of detail that matters on character homes in Newtown, where a hundred-year-old front door is often the first thing a visitor actually looks at. Getting it right takes the same care as a much bigger job, just aimed at a much smaller surface.
