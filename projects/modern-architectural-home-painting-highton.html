A Complete Repaint for a Home Built Around Curves

A contemporary Highton home with sweeping curved walls and a strong architectural silhouette, repainted inside and out with careful attention to its distinctive lines, surfaces and details.

Category
Exterior & Interior Painting
Property
Contemporary Multi-Level Home
Location
Highton, VIC

The Challenge

Nowhere for an Uneven Line to Hide

This Highton home isn't built on straight lines. The upper level curves and cantilevers out over a darker rendered podium wall below, with an angled support column, an open balcony, and long uninterrupted stretches of pale rendered wall running the full height of the building.

That kind of minimalist, sculptural design is unforgiving of anything less than a clean finish. There's no pattern, no texture, no busy detailing to distract the eye — just the shape of the building itself. A slightly uneven line on a curve, or a rough patch on an otherwise flawless wall, would be far more obvious here than on an ordinary home.

The project wasn't limited to the exterior either. The main living areas inside the home were repainted as part of the same job, giving the owners a fresh interior to match the newly finished exterior.

Scope of Work

Exterior Painting Scope

  • Curved and angular rendered walls finished in a light, neutral tone
  • The lower podium wall finished in a contrasting dark grey render
  • Balcony structure detailed as part of the exterior finish
  • Surrounding landscaping, windows and hard surfaces protected throughout
  • Interior repaint of the home's main living areas

Our Approach

Treating a Feature Home Like a Feature Home

A home with this much architectural personality doesn't get the standard treatment. Every stage was approached with the finished silhouette in mind, since it's the kind of job a genuine residential exterior painting service should be built around, not just squeezed into a routine day.

01

Assess the existing surfaces

Every wall, curve and level was assessed before starting, given how differently the rendered surfaces behave compared to a standard flat facade.

02

Prepare the rendered areas

The rendered walls were prepared ahead of painting, since imperfections in render show through far more obviously on a home with this little visual distraction.

03

Address surface imperfections

Any imperfections identified in the render were addressed before the topcoat went on, rather than being painted straight over.

04

Protect the surroundings

The landscaping, windows and paved and concrete areas around the home were protected throughout the project.

05

Hold the line on every curve

Clean edges were maintained around the balcony, the podium wall junction and every curved transition on the building.

06

Finish for consistency

The exterior coating was applied for an even, consistent finish across every level and surface of the home.

07

Repaint the main living areas

Inside, the home's main living areas were repainted as part of the same project, giving the interior the same fresh, considered finish as the exterior.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is paint quality more noticeable on architecturally designed homes?

Minimalist, architecturally designed homes usually have large, uninterrupted wall surfaces with no pattern or texture to distract the eye, so any unevenness in the paint finish tends to stand out far more than it would on a more conventional facade.

How do painters maintain clean lines on curved or angular walls?

Curved and angular sections need to be approached with extra care around every transition and edge, since a slightly uneven line is much more visible on a sweeping curve than on a straight wall.

Does rendered surface preparation matter more on minimalist homes?

Yes. On a large, plain rendered wall, any surface imperfection left unaddressed before painting will likely show through the finished coat, so proper preparation is especially important on this style of home.

Why protect landscaping and hard surfaces during an exterior repaint?

Protecting garden beds, windows and paved or concrete areas throughout the job helps keep the finished result clean and avoids unnecessary clean-up or damage to established landscaping around the property.

Can a light, neutral exterior colour still make a bold architectural statement?

Often, yes. On a home with strong architectural lines and curves, a light, neutral colour can let the shape of the building itself do the talking, rather than competing with it for attention.

Can a repaint cover both the exterior and interior of a home in one project?

Yes, it's a common approach for homeowners wanting to refresh a property in one go. Coordinating the exterior and interior work together also means the finish across the whole home feels considered, rather than done in separate, unrelated stages.

The Result

Letting the Architecture Do the Talking

The finished exterior gives this Highton home exactly what a design like this needs — a consistent, clean surface that supports the architecture rather than competing with it. The light render lets every curve and angle read clearly, while the darker podium wall grounds the building visually against the driveway and landscaping below.

It's a good example of what a considered exterior painters in Highton team can bring to a property where the design itself is the main event — the goal was never to add colour for its own sake, but to protect and refresh the surfaces without dulling the character that makes the home stand out in the first place.

The interior repaint of the main living areas rounded the project out, giving the owners a home that feels considered from the street right through to the rooms they actually spend their time in.
